Case Study · Product Analytics

Migrating 750 self-serve users from Amplitude to Mixpanel for 19% less.

Our Amplitude contract scaled poorly as more employees requested access, and numbers had drifted away from Snowflake. By routing events through our existing Segment layer into Mixpanel, we migrated 750 internal users with zero app releases and cut annual spend from $220K to $179K.

01 — The Business Driver

Giving 750 employees self-serve product analytics without runaway event costs

Product managers, CRM specialists, and marketers needed to query user behavior on their own, without the contract cost spiking every time traffic grew.

NEED

Reliable daily self-service

Joyn grew from 5–7M monthly users in 2021 to around 20M. Teams across the company needed a single product analytics tool they could open daily and trust against warehouse numbers.

GAP

Per-event billing penalized identity resolution

Amplitude billed per event. As browser cookies became shorter-lived, users were re-identified on almost every visit, and every identify call burned through our event quota.

What was at stake
Billing per event = cost scales with traffic volume
$220K projected = budget spent on repeat identify calls
02 — The Obstacles

Runaway event volume, low trust in numbers, and zero client engineering capacity

We had to cut the annual run-rate, reconcile user counts against Snowflake, and switch tools without asking app teams for a single client release.

COST

Identify calls inflated event volume

Every anonymous-to-registered user pairing and session start triggered an identify event. Misconfigured client calls fired multiple times per session, consuming our event quota without adding analytical value.

Duplicate identify calls = budget burned on redundant pings
TRUST

Drifting metrics and no formal onboarding

Reported active user counts regularly diverged from Snowflake, and most product teams had never been trained on how to build or interpret funnels accurately.

Untrusted numbers = product decisions delayed or made on gut feel
CAPACITY

No room on the client engineering roadmap

Re-instrumenting SDKs across mobile, web, and smart TV apps would have stalled product delivery for months. Because Segment already sat in the middle, we had a server-side path available.

Client SDK changes = months of blocked app releases
03 — The Solution

Negotiate an MTU contract, switch destinations in Segment, and reconcile daily with Snowflake

We executed the migration in four steps: commercial modeling, a three-phase Segment cutover, automated daily Snowflake reconciliation, and a year-long onboarding program.

1
Model event vs. MTU pricing Amplitude vs Mixpanel

I led the technical evaluation, negotiated directly with both vendors, and modeled our three-year cost under event-based versus Monthly Tracked User (MTU) pricing. Under Mixpanel's MTU model, identity resolution calls were free. The Head of Product Ops signed off on the business case and budget.

Cost model · Vendor negotiation
2
Cut over through Segment in three waves foundation → depth → marketing

Because client events already flowed through Segment, we didn't need a single app or web release. Working with a data engineer, warehouse specialist, product analyst, the Head of Reporting, and a Mixpanel implementation engineer, we rolled out core screen, identity, and playback events first, followed by rail impression and click events, and finally marketing attribution models.

Segment · Backend uploads
3
Automate daily Snowflake reconciliation Mixpanel ↔ Snowflake

We built an automated daily Prefect job comparing Mixpanel active user counts against our Snowflake warehouse tables. Early variance swung between 2% and 19%; daily monitoring and schema fixes brought the gap down to roughly 5%.

Prefect · Snowflake
4
Run training and governance for a full year training → support → shared definitions

Following initial group workshops, I ran internal enablement for twelve months: bi-weekly Friday office hours, first-line Slack support, an internal FAQ built from real support threads, and a governed Mixpanel Lexicon so every event and property had a clear business owner and definition.

Office hours · Slack · Lexicon

05 — Strategic Outcomes Unlocked

How Mixpanel became Joyn's daily product analytics workspace

⚡

Adopted across engineering, product, data science, and marketing

Engineers monitor error rates, funnel drop-offs, and feature-flag rollouts. PMs run discovery and experiment checks. Data scientists inspect A/B test distributions, and marketing tracks campaign cohorts on the same event definitions.

⊕

Editorial teams manage homepage rails with live data

Content curators check lane click-through performance daily and weekly to promote or rotate shows on the home screen.

↻

Baked into new-hire onboarding

Mixpanel training is now a standard onboarding module for every new engineer, product manager, and content curator joining Joyn.

✕

Governed definitions in Lexicon

Documented event schemas in Lexicon paired with the daily Snowflake reconciliation check eliminated recurring debates over whose dashboard had the right number.

⟲

Managing cost at 20M+ monthly users

As Joyn scaled to 20M monthly users, we introduced 30% user sampling to cut licensing costs by ~70% versus 100% ingestion, balancing budget guardrails against PM requests for unsampled cohort depth.
